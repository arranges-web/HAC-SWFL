How much a typical AC service visit should cost
Service calls vary based on what is needed. Use these common scenarios as a guide, not a guarantee:
Drain line clog and safety switch reset: $120 to $250
Dual run capacitor replacement: $150 to $350
Contactor replacement: $175 to $350
Blower motor replacement: $450 to $950 for standard PSC, higher for ECM variable speed
Refrigerant leak search with dye or nitrogen test: $250 to $600
Small coil leak repair, if accessible: $300 to $750
Refrigerant charge correction: price depends on refrigerant type and amount; modern systems with R-410A often run $100 to $200 per pound including labor; legacy R-22 is higher and usually signals time to
discuss replacement
Every estimate should include labor, parts, and any warranty information. Ask what is covered and for how long. A fair company provides a written breakdown and does not push parts that do not solve the problem.
How long an AC repair should take
Simple fixes, like a capacitor, contactor, float switch, or cleared drain: 45 to 90 minutes
Thermostat replacement and setup: 45 to 90 minutes
Blower motor or fan motor replacement: 1.5 to 3 hours
Leak search and repair: 2 to 5 hours, sometimes split across two visits
Compressor replacement: 4 to 8 hours, plus system evacuation and recharge
Delays usually come from part sourcing, rusted or seized fasteners, warranty verification, or access constraints in an attic or tight air handler closet. A good technician explains the timeline before starting.
What drives the price up or down
Age and condition: Older systems have higher failure rates and harder to find parts
Refrigerant type: R-22 systems cost more to recharge; R-410A is more manageable
Brand and model: Proprietary parts and inverter systems can run higher
Warranty status: Manufacturer or extended warranties reduce part cost but labor may still apply
Location and access: Attics and tight closets add labor time
Seasonal demand: Peak summer heat leads to higher volume; reputable companies still hold pricing steady and schedule swiftly
When to repair versus replace
Use this simple framework:
Under 8 years old, minor single part failure, efficient system: repair
8 to 12 years old, multiple failures in 12 months, rising energy bills: price both paths
12 to 15 plus years old, compressor or coil failure, R-22 refrigerant, uneven cooling: consider replacement
If repair costs exceed 25 to 40 percent of a new high efficiency system, replacement often makes more financial sense, especially with utility savings and financing availability. Our installations can start as low as $38 per
month, which is useful when an older system faces a major component failure.
How to spot unreasonable charges
Vague estimates, no line items, or pressure to decide on the spot without questions
Large repair quotes that shift after you agree
Claims that your system is unsafe without proof, photos, or meter readings
Charging for a diagnostic even after you approve the repair, unless disclosed beforehand
Look for technicians who test first, show readings, and explain options. Ask what happens if the repair does not fix the problem. We stand behind our diagnostics and provide clear next steps.
